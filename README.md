# Deepdelve

A first-person, real-time dungeon crawler for mobile browsers, built in the spirit of the
1993 seed-generated dungeon classics. Pick a class, roll your stats, type a seed, and descend.

**Play it now:** https://dungeon-hack-mobile.vercel.app

Open it on your phone and choose "Add to Home Screen" to install it as an app. The title shows when the game is ready offline. Voice recordings download separately in the background and resume after reconnecting.

## Features

- **The Wayfarer, a hidden Spellblade.** Tap the release label at the bottom of
  the title five times, then choose **Spellblade** in New Game. The unlock stays
  on this device, supports keyboard/controller activation, and never replaces a
  save. This is an Easter egg, not private account access. The six ordinary
  classes, Quick Start and shared Daily/Weekly heroes stay unchanged.
  Intelligence guides the sword. Runic Edge adds fire, frost or lightning to a
  sword or dagger for thirty seconds; Lantern Step crosses up to two clear,
  explored floor squares ahead; Spellguard catches one incoming hit for 2.2
  seconds and grants +3 on the next landed blade hit within five seconds.
  Beyond floor eight, the held lantern gains +1 armour at floor 9, another
  every two floors, capped at +4 (Deepward).
  Runes replace each other, magic shares attack recovery, and poison and hunger
  pass through the guard. The class wears light armour, carries its own silver
  lantern, and gains six exclusive talents plus lantern lessons rather than paths or a Mythic trial.
  Remember the gatekeeper's oath through Deathless Inscription, Sever the Veil,
  The Forgotten Road, Threshold Ward, Hold Back the Night and The Debt Repaid.
  Each talent also adds five maximum hit points and two maximum spell points,
  restoring those amounts. Sever the Veil guides an active runed blade with +1 to hit.
  The Silver Lantern is visible in the left hand throughout the dungeon, with its blue flame.
  His own gatekeeper origin and motive appear before descent; the shared background
  still supplies its chosen starting modifiers. Older Wayfarer saves retain their
  learned lessons and receive the new choices on future levels.
  Its painted portrait, pixel fallback, rune clocks and class survive offline saves.

- **A focused first run.** Choose any class and descend with the existing eight-floor,
  one-life defaults. Backgrounds, rolled or bought scores, seed and dungeon settings
  sit under **Customise hero & dungeon**; mastery goals have their own disclosure.
  Folding settings preserves choices. The Journal starts on **Quests** and groups
  Pages, Bestiary, Companions, Relics and Combinations under **Stories & collections**.
  Attack names its existing cooldown as Ready or Recovering, enemy warning marks
  keep a readable exclamation before filling, and misses read **MISS**. Pack names
  use four columns on narrow phones (three at large text), with the same 20 slots;
  Compact view rounds to the displayed columns.
  The map key expands under **Map symbols**; Rest names its life recovery and its
  accessible description includes spell points, food and interruption risk.

- **Remembered places and travelling companions.** Entered set pieces receive a
  short place card and a name in the map's Known landmarks and the run recap.
  Unvisited rooms stay hidden. Quests includes optional field notes on warbands,
  packs and mixed melee/ranged fights. Companion portraits show short reactions
  to the place, wounds and victory, plus separate learned and upcoming skill cards
  using their existing floor milestones. The end screen highlights one optional
  goal for the next hero. Recorded impacts retain bone, slime, metal or spectral
  responses; the Dark Elf Halls, Grey Hold, marsh, drowned halls and old forges each
  have their own synthesised ambience, alongside the existing sound pack.

- **Painted relic portraits.** All 47 named relics have paintings in the discovered
  Relic codex and identified item details. Tap a painting, or focus it and press
  Enter or Space, to enlarge it. Portraits are cached for offline viewing.

- **Optional dungeon puzzles.** At the first stair of every new custom or Quick
  Start run, tick **Include dungeon puzzles**. It starts unticked for each hero.
  Rune sequences, watchfire patterns and keeper-seal deductions guard some existing
  gold and gems, with clues beside their controls. Stand on a cache or face it and
  press **Puzzle** (Use); the dungeon pauses while it is open and keyboard focus stays inside the dialog. **Start again** clears
  a guess, and **Leave for later** keeps your progress. There are unlimited retries,
  no wrong-answer damage. Once opened, keep its gold and gems or, when the
  purse covers it, choose a Potion of Healing or class-usable unidentified gear
  instead of some coins. Remaining coins come with the item. Choose once, then
  collect the share from the floor; a full pack never destroys it. The lid lifts
  once with a short sound; Calm view and reduced-motion settings show a still
  open cache. Keys, relics, quest objects, passages,
  stairs and the Heart are never gated. Progress travels in saves, save codes and
  same-build replays. Daily and Weekly challenges keep puzzles off so their rules
  stay fixed. Floors without a suitable treasure pile, and the final floor, have no cache.

- **Watch a recorded delve.** New runs record their actions and timing locally. After
  victory or defeat, choose **Replay this run** to watch the first-person journey,
  pause it or play at up to 4× speed. Playback leaves saves, trophies and Daily results
  untouched and works offline once cached. Records continue with saved games; older,
  imported, testing-aid or over-limit runs do not offer replay. Playback requires the
  same build and is currently available from the finished run's end screen. If storage
  is tight, the optional recording is dropped before retrying the game save.
  See [recording details and limits](docs/REPLAY.md).

- **A welcome on the title.** Unread News comes first; otherwise a random game fact
  appears. Dismiss it to unlock the theme music, subject to your sound settings.
  The release and build are shown at the bottom of the main page.
- **Seeded dungeons.** Every level is generated from your seed string, so the same seed always
  gives the same dungeon. Share seeds with friends.
- **Customisable descent.** Choose the number of levels (2 to 16), map size, monster density,
  treasure density, locked doors and keys, traps, and permadeath, just like the original's
  dungeon-customisation screen. A difficulty picker at the top sets Easy, Normal or Hard.
- **One life by default.** Permadeath is on unless you untick it. The run is still saved when
  you change levels or put the game away, so Continue picks it up, but Load stays shut and a
  death erases the save.
- **The Daily Delve.** One dungeon a day, the same for everyone: the seed, the class, the
  background and the scores all come from the date. Eight floors, normal difficulty, one life,
  one try. The title then shows how it went, the Hall marks the run, a streak counts the days in
  a row, and Share copies a one-line result. Nothing leaves the device.
  A second, the Ranger & Druid Daily, deals one of those two classes, with its own record and streak.
- **A story you descend into.** An opening sets up the valley above Karrathal and what went wrong
  beneath it. Each of the eight levels holds one page left by the guild crews who went first, and
  the Journal collects them as the account of what actually happened down there assembles itself.
- **Ten backgrounds.** The Oathbroken, Tombwise, Ashborn, Cloistered, Deep-born and Debtor. Each
  gives a lasting advantage, a paragraph of the opening, and the line your run closes on. Two more
  are earned: The Returned opens after any win on Normal or Hard, The Heartsworn after a win on
  Hard. The Daily Delve only ever deals the first six.
- **Trophies and a relic codex.** Progress that outlasts a run, kept on the device under one key.
  Each ordinary class earns a trophy for each difficulty it wins (daily runs included),
  shown as a grid at the top of the Hall of Heroes, and the victory screen says when one is new.
  Every relic any hero picks up or buys goes in the codex, readable from the Hall or the
  Journal's Relics tab; the rest show only whether they are a weapon, armour or a shield.
- **A choice at every level.** A lesson at most levels, three offered at a time, most of them a rule (an opening blow that cannot miss, a floor's first trap stepped over, no grip holds you, every potion known, the stairs on the map) and a few a number; on every even level a class talent instead, one of three from the class's own six or seven, each changing how the class fights, and a talent passed over is held for the next offer.
  Self-Taught can be taken twice; the rest only once.
- **A path at level 3.** Each ordinary class chooses one of two paths, for good, in place of that level's
  lesson (or at the divided stair, if it comes first): Knight or Berserker, Templar or Healer, Pyromancer or Frostweaver, Assassin or Trickster.
  Each changes two or three rules (a shield that catches blows, rage that grows with your wounds,
  fire that keeps burning, a slip aside from a blow) and some give something up for it. The path
  is on the Hero sheet, the end screen and the Hall of Heroes.
- **Mastery at level 7.** A hero on a path masters it, again in place of the lesson: one of the
  path's two capstones, each one rule (a Knight's shield that staggers what it catches a blow from,
  a Berserker who will not fall once a floor, a Pyromancer's fire that deals double to what already
  burns, a Sharpshooter whose arrow at three squares cannot miss).
- **Six classes** with AD&D-flavoured rules: Fighter, Cleric, Mage, Thief, Ranger, Druid. Six ability scores
  (4d6 drop lowest), hit dice, armour class, to-hit progression, class weapon and armour limits,
  experience levels up to 12. Each has its own way to stay alive: fighters are hardy and recover
  faster, clerics heal, mages kill at range, thieves dodge, crit often and backstab, rangers keep
  their distance, and druids take a bear's shape, whose hide takes the blows first (any other
  spell lets it go). All six can be chosen from the start.
- **Heroes with a past.** Each class has its own backstory on the selection screen.
  Select a class, then tap its portrait to expand the artwork. The six hero
  paintings are refreshed with quieter dungeon backgrounds. The Lich, Warlord
  and Heartforged use the bestiary's creature compositions, equipment and
  dungeon settings, shared with their introductions.
- **Six Mythic bargains.** One signature piece for each ordinary class, usable on either
  path, with its own power, drawback and lavender artwork. Only one Mythic may
  be worn at a time. In custom delves of at least eight floors, the final Vigil
  Lamp offers an optional extra ogre fight after its usual aid, at its usual cost. Defeat the
  guardian for your class's piece; a full pack leaves it safely on the floor.
  Daily/Weekly and shorter delves keep their current rules. Mythics are never
  sold by traders or offered as mastery starting gear.
- **Landmarks and treasure reveals.** Carved stair jambs, shrine compass inlays
  and the final sanctum's glass floorwork use cached art, with the same fog,
  light and clear stair openings. Open the pack after finding a Legendary or
  Mythic piece to see its larger artwork, powers and lore. The brief reveal
  respects Calm view and reduced motion; finding treasure never opens a popup
  or pauses combat. Taking up a save does not repeat old reveals.
- **Named treasure artwork.** Every Mythic, legendary piece and named relic has its own lore-led
  picture in the pack, treasure inspection and discovered codex, and matching
  held weapons, bows, shields and foci. Ordinary enchanted gear keeps its base art.
- **A painted bestiary.** Every creature has portrait art in the bestiary and Journal,
  with an enlarged view and a sprite fallback if the painting cannot load.
  Animated creatures and fallback hero faces match the paintings' identities,
  anatomy and equipment while keeping their original animation style. Refined creature
  sprites include clearer canine and wyrm silhouettes, connected crossbows, shell
  shields, dimensional mimics and distinct spellcasting poses.
- **Melee and missile arms.** Throwing knives, slings and bows reach down a corridor, so archers
  and casters are not the only ones with an answer at range. Monsters move faster than they
  swing, so a missile weapon buys you a few shots rather than an endless retreat.
- **Real-time combat.** Monsters wake, path toward you and attack on their own clocks. A door
  pulled shut matters: whatever has hands opens it, but beasts must batter it down over several
  seconds (you hear every blow), and ogres, trolls and minotaurs smash it to splinters, for good. Fifty-three monster types, and ten named champions, including undead, poisoners, a regenerating troll, a
  basilisk whose gaze turns whoever meets it to stone, a rustmaw that eats your armour's
  enchantment, a hound that steps out of the air at your back, a quillback whose raised quills
  punish the blow struck into them, a wyrm whose fire runs down the passage but not under its
  jaws, and a life-draining boss guarding the artifact on the deepest level. Each of the first four
  floors has a creature of its own whose answer is not a step back (a moth whose flare dazzles,
  a quilled hog, an imp that comes back at your back, a goblin with a pike), so those lessons are
  first met where a mistake costs little. Which of
  them a floor holds goes by how far through the delve it is, so a short delve meets them all.
  Every trick is warned of, armour does not turn a warned blow, and answering one leaves an opening.
- **A boss fight in three acts.** The Dread Lich speaks when it wakes, and its life runs across
  the top of the view. At two thirds it raises skeleton guards and steps back behind them to
  throw grave-cold over their heads; at one third it puts out every torch in its hall,
  quickens, and tries to drink the Heart's light to mend itself, a rite any wound breaks.
  A mage has answers of their own: a spell pulls the lich's shadow ward apart and gives back a
  third of their spell points, and grave-cold breaks on a mage's Shield.
- **Blows that leave a mark.** Every monster bleeds its own colour, from red to a slime's
  green to a wraith's cold light; heavy blows and kills stain the floor, misses strike sparks,
  and a hard hit on you jolts the view and leaves blood on its edges. Poison, frost, webs and
  blessings each tint the view their own way, and timed effects count down.
- **Magic.** Mage and Cleric spell lists (bolts, buffs, heals) with spell points, plus scrolls
  anyone can read.
- **Items.** Weapons, armour and shields with enchantments, potions, scrolls, food, gems, gold,
  and colour-coded keys for locked doors.
- **Oils.** Fire Oil, Silver Wash, Blade Venom and Frost Oil coat the weapon for its next 20 blows that land:
  more fire damage (and no troll mending), more against the undead, poison for the living, or cold
  that holds the foe back a moment and freezes the water round it.
- **Regional architecture.** Crypts have carved burial masonry and long ledger stones;
  the Grey Hold has iron uprights, geometric paving and metal-clad doors; the marsh
  has muddy ground, reed litter and rope-bound weathered wood. Elven halls, the
  Sanctum, drowned halls and old forges have their own inlays, stains and ironwork.
  Details are painted into cached textures, with the existing light and fog ranges,
  lock colours, water cues and Calm view preserved. Broken ledger lids, discarded
  ironwork, crystal shards, roots and reed bundles dress the existing scenery
  positions without adding obstacles or changing generation.
- **Finishing touches.** Creatures follow through after a blow and ease out of a
  flinch; sleeping creatures breathe more slowly. Valuable finds catch a small
  glint, with known rare and legendary pieces in their own colours, and selecting
  an item in the pack shows its framed artwork, grade and usual actions together.
  Hidden quality stays hidden. Force darts, thorn whips, roots, holy seals,
  healing motes, shields, blessings and a bear's paw give magic distinct shapes.
  Calm view (also the default for reduced motion) omits the new reaction poses,
  glints and travelling magic, retaining small, still impacts which fade away.
  Character, bestiary and inventory screens share rounded frames and selection marks.
- **A trader in the dark.** One of the Lampfolk (on a goblin market floor, a goblin pedlar) sets up shop on about two floors in five, selling
  potions, scrolls, food and the odd weapon, and buying whatever you do not want for about a quarter
  of what they would ask. Buying something identifies it. Gold you never spend is just a number on your
  gravestone.
- **Two last fights.** The Crypts end at the Dread Lich; the Warrens at the Goblin Warlord, who
  beats a war-drum for his warband, takes to his throne behind two shield-bearers at two thirds,
  and fights in a frenzy at one third.
- **Jobs from the traders.** A trader may offer a job for the floor below: slay its champion,
  cull its creatures, or find a lost satchel. Free to take, for that floor only, and paid in gold
  and a flask or scroll by the next trader you meet.
- **A hound at your heel.** On floor 2 of most delves a starving hound watches from the dark.
  Share your food, or win it over with a word, and it follows you down: it bites whatever
  stands beside it (the thing at your side first), draws the blows of anything that reaches it
  before you, heals when you rest and grows with you, and leaves a fight to follow you if you
  walk on. It finds its own way round doors and crowds, shows on both maps, and stands beside
  you on the picture of a won run. Walk into it to swap places; face it and
  press Use to tell it to stay or call it to heel. It is no help to a quiet step: sleepers hear a hero
  with a hound at heel a square sooner. A hound told to stay is left behind on the
  stair. If it falls it is gone for the run, and the epilogue remembers it.
- **Or a goblin with clever fingers.** A delve with no hound keeps a caged goblin about halfway
  down. Let it out and it follows as the hound does, but it is a poor fighter and a quiet one:
  it picks the locks you have no key for, makes safe the traps it passes, and lends its fingers
  to an encounter's Dexterity test (+2). One companion a run.
- **Companions remembered.** Open **Companions** from the title screen or the
  Journal to view portraits and descriptions of the six companion types your
  heroes have met. Meeting one unlocks it even if you do not recruit it; unknown
  types stay hidden. Discoveries persist on this device across delves, portraits
  can be enlarged by touch or keyboard, and all class portraits work offline. Loading older
  saves recovers discoveries from their companion and recorded encounters.
- **Companions that grow.** Every new floor a companion comes down at your side counts: blooded
  after two, a veteran after four, tougher each time and with a trick of its kind (a hound's
  hamstring and pack hunting; a goblin's backstab and scrounging). A charm from a trader (a studded
  collar, a fang, a rowan knot) makes it tougher, fiercer or slowly self-mending.
- **Survival.** Hunger, poison (fought off with a Constitution save), traps in corridors (dodged
  with a Dexterity save; a pit is only halved). Wounds close on their own only up to half
  your life; past that it takes a potion, a prayer or a rest. Resting costs food, is blocked by
  nearby enemies (the Rest button dims and says so; it never drinks for you), and thins out: the first rest on a floor restores up to two thirds of your life, the next a third,
  the third a quarter, and then the dark is too close to sleep. Potions are found rarely enough
  that the gold you carry has something to buy.
- **An ending that is a fight.** The Heart of the Mountain will not come loose while the Dread
  Lich stands. Bring it down, lift the Heart, and its light floods the view and carries you out:
  the run is won there and then, with no long walk back.
- **Unknown potions and scrolls.** Every dungeon shuffles appearances, so a "cloudy potion" is
  a different draught in each seed. Drink or read it to learn what it is.
- **Champions.** Feral, Armoured, Ancient and Rabid monsters appear more often as you descend, and each
  mark changes how one fights: a Feral one charges whatever its kind, an Armoured one's plates turn the
  first blow, an Ancient one uses its trick twice as often, and a Rabid one never flees.
  They hit harder, take more killing, and always drop something worth having.
- **Fire, cold and lightning.** Spells, the Scrolls of Fire, Frost and Lightning, a flaming or frost-oiled blade and burns each carry an
  element, and some kinds take half as much again from one and only half from another (slimes,
  spiders, zombies, trolls and quillbacks burn well; the dead barely feel the cold; rustmaws and
  bats draw the lightning; a cave wyrm shrugs off fire and fears the cold). The first hit that finds out says so and the bestiary keeps it. The wraith's touch
  and the lich's grave-cold are cold, and a Ring of Warmth halves them.
- **Named champions.** About a third and two thirds of the way down, one floor each is held by
  a named foe, chosen by the seed from those that suit the depth: Grisk the Goblin King, Vessra
  the Web-Mother, Ushgar the Orc Warchief, Morrow the Ghoul Lord, Orla the Hollow Abbess and
  Gorrum the Troll-Father. Each is its kind grown great and washed in its own colour, asleep in
  a lair away from the stairs with some of its kin, named in the log when you arrive, and given
  a life bar across the top of the view once awake. Each sharpens its kind's trick (a horn that
  calls kin at half health, webs or a claw twice as often, a hand that drinks your life), warned
  of and answered like every other. It falls for a relic the traders were keeping for your
  class, or a +2 piece and gold once they have none left, and the bestiary, end screen and Hall
  of Heroes remember it.
- **Your dead come back.** The last hero lost on a device (outside the Daily Delve) is
  remembered there. A later run (never a daily one) finds their bones on the floor where they fell, still wearing their gear,
  with their shade risen over them: made to that floor, fighting as its class did, and warned of
  in the log on arrival. Laying it to rest wins the gear (quality hidden again, curses and all,
  relics left behind) and forgets them; the Hall records who was laid to rest. The map is the
  seed's own either way: the bones are placed afterwards, from a stream of their own.
- **The deep answers strength.** A hero who arrives on a floor well ahead of the usual level
  finds it readier for them: its creatures take more killing, hit surer and harder, and more of
  them are champions. A hero on pace or behind finds each floor as it was made.
- **Torchlit halls.** Wall brackets cast real pools of light across floors, walls and monsters,
  each flame flickering on its own rhythm. Every depth decorates its walls its own way: iron
  rings and candle niches, skulls and ossuaries in the catacombs, moss and roots in the damp,
  frost and grates in the vaults, chains in the crypts, glowing runes in the sanctum.
- **Secrets.** Hidden doors lead to treasure vaults. Search suspicious walls, or play a Thief and
  spot them in passing. Wall fountains restore you fully, once per fountain.
- **Ranged foes.** Goblin archers and dark acolytes attack down corridors, so cover matters.
  Wounded monsters break and flee, and a strong character can shoulder a locked door open.
- **A map you can read.** Explored ground is cropped and scaled to fill the screen, the player
  is a ringed square with a facing arrow, stairs, doors, locked doors, fountains and traders each
  have their own colour and symbol, and a legend names them all. A live corner minimap too.
  Discovery stops at the view's fog limit; unseen ground stays hidden, and both the corner
  and docked maps wait until the opening passage ends.
- **You are told what is happening.** Attacks name the direction they came from, the edge of the
  view flashes on that side, walking into a wall says so, and the death screen names your killer
  and replays your last moments. Win or lose, the end screen sums up the run: your best blow,
  the hardest hit you took, what you killed and how many, the talents and relics you carried.
- **Nothing is spent for nothing.** Food, potions, scrolls and spells refuse to be used when they
  could not help, and say why. Unidentified draughts are always usable, since refusing one would
  tell you what it is.
- **Automap** of explored areas plus a live corner minimap, message log with full history,
  monster health bars, save/load to local storage with autosave on every level change, and a
  Hall of Heroes that remembers your best runs. The pack compares any weapon or armour against
  what you are already wearing, in damage per second or armour class. Equipment surrounds
  a compact neutral silhouette shared by all classes: weapon by the right hand,
  armour at the chest, and one left-hand slot for a shield, focus or second weapon.
  Selecting an item highlights its valid equipment slots; its details open in a bottom
  sheet with actions kept in reach. Escape or controller Back closes the details first.
  Tap a slot to browse compatible replacements; filters separate weapons, worn gear
  and supplies. A compact bag option hides unused rows while keeping all 20 squares.
  New finds stay marked until inspected, including across reloads; a growing stack
  is marked again. Before/after item stats and weapon-rate estimates share their
  presentation with read-only shop inspection and the discovered relic collection.
  Both-hand previews explain shields, second blades and two-handed grips before equipping.
  Under an item's **Favourites and quick-use belt**, protect it from dropping and selling,
  or pin food, potions and scrolls to three dungeon controls. Depleted belt slots retain their
  assignments for later refills. These preferences belong to the current run on this device.
  The Journal's **Haul** lists actual floor pickups, excluding purchases, gifts and dropped
  items taken back; earlier pickups from old saves are not reconstructed. Relic sets show
  discovery and worn progress separately, and the Companions page shows the current
  companion's health, learned abilities and following, waiting or fallen state.
  Search carried items by name, public type or a known power; search combines with
  the category and equipment-slot filters without identifying hidden gear.
  The Journal's **Quests** shows job progress, payment instructions and unfinished
  leads. Tap a status chip for **Conditions**, with its effect and remaining duration.
  **Recap**, also available after the run ends, brings together notable finds,
  paid jobs, companion milestones and defeated bosses. Follow/Stay buttons beside
  the companion portrait use the same facing and reach restrictions as **Use**.
  The map lists known traders, shrines, locked doors and unresolved encounters;
  select a landmark to centre it. Resolved encounter markers are remembered on this
  device from this update onward, and unexplored places stay hidden.
  Large text uses a readable two-column equipment list.
- **Sound.** Placed by ear: a blow drawn back on your left is heard on your left, and a far one
  is quieter and duller. Blades, clubs, arrows, fists and spells each strike differently, armour
  and shields ring, each kind of creature has its own voice and its own death, and every spell
  its own sound. Each floor has its own drone and distant noises (drips, chains, wind), which
  tighten while the lich is fighting, and you hear your own heartbeat once badly wounded.
- **George narrates the story.** The opening and backgrounds, found Journal pages, first region
  and relic discoveries, and death/victory epilogues have recorded British narration. Voice is on
  by default, with pause, replay, skip and its own volume and switch. Discoveries wait for quiet
  and stop for combat warnings. Both audio formats are saved in the background; the title says when all voice recordings are ready offline. The game can already be played offline while they download.
- **Music that follows the fight.** Written as it plays, in each floor's own scale: a few bell
  notes and long silences while nothing is near, a low pulse when something awake is close, a
  quicker beat and a repeating figure once it comes to blows, and a horn beneath for a named
  champion, a shade or the lich. When the fight ends it comes home to the floor's own note and
  then hushes a while. It has its own switch in the Menu.
- **A living title screen.** The menu sits over a real generated dungeon with a ghost camera
  walking it, drawn by the same raycaster as the game, with drifting embers and torch flicker.
- **Mobile first.** Big touch d-pad with hold-to-walk, swipe on the view to turn or step, tap
  it to act, haptic feedback, portrait and landscape layouts, safe-area aware, installable as a
  PWA with offline support. Keyboard controls on desktop.

The code is plain ES modules with no compilation: the page loads `js/main.js` and the browser
resolves the rest, so what you edit is what ships. Types are written as JSDoc and checked by
TypeScript in `--noEmit` mode, which catches renamed fields and bad shapes without putting a
compiler between the source and the browser. Everything is original: the raycast renderer
with textured floors and ceilings, procedural
wall textures, pixel-art sprites and sound effects are all generated in code. No external
assets or dependencies. Creatures are drawn as 24x24 tone art; the outline, contact shadow and
overhead light are applied at load time so every sprite reads the same way against a dark wall.

## Mythic class items

The final Vigil Lamp in custom delves of eight floors or more offers an optional
trial. Preview the bargain, choose the lamp's normal aid at its normal price (or
none), then defeat its guardian. Cancelling the preview spends nothing. The
reward belongs to your class and works with either path; only one Mythic can be
worn. Each has its own picture, spoken lore and a cost to weigh against its power.

| Class | Mythic | Power | Drawback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fighter | The Last Gate | Blocks 2 from ordinary close blows; stores up to 3 for the next close hit within five seconds | −1 armour class |
| Cleric | Mercy's Oath | Healing spells mend 25% more | Damaging spells cost 1 extra spell point |
| Mage | The Bloodstar | Damaging spells deal 20% more | Each costs 2 life, bypassing wards; unavailable at 2 life or less |
| Thief | Nightglass | Close hits add 2 damage, or 6 from the shadows | −1 armour class |
| Ranger | Horizon | Arrows from at least two squares add 4 damage | Draw takes 15% longer |
| Druid | The Elderseed | Bear's base hide gains 4; Entangle's base duration gains one second | Healing spells mend 20% less |

## Deploy on Vercel

The game is a static site. The included `vercel.json` runs a small commit-stamping
step and configures service-worker caching and security headers; game code is not compiled.

**From the dashboard**

1. Push this repository to GitHub.
2. In Vercel, click **Add New → Project** and import the repository.
3. Leave **Framework Preset** as **Other**, use the build command from `vercel.json`, and leave the output
   directory as the repository root.
4. Click **Deploy**. Every push to the default branch redeploys automatically.

**From the CLI**

```bash
npm i -g vercel
vercel          # preview deployment
vercel --prod   # production deployment
```

**Automatic versions and GitHub releases**

For each update, stage the intended changes, then run:

```bash
npm run release:prepare -- --message "feat: describe the new feature"
git add package.json package-lock.json js/release.js sw.js
```

Small additions, fixes and polish default to a **patch**, including `feat:` commits.
Choose `--bump minor` for a substantial gameplay feature or sizeable content batch;
reserve `--bump major` for a major overhaul approved by the owner. Commit labels
alone never select a larger milestone. Group small changes into a release batch.
The title footer shows the release version and the deployed seven-character Git
commit ID. Vercel stamps the full commit into the page during deployment with
`node stamp-commit.cjs`; the local server stamps its checkout in memory. The same
identity is retained offline and used in opt-in reports. Internal release and
offline cache counters still increase with each prepared release. Docs, tests and CI-only changes leave
the numbers unchanged. Repository agent instructions require preparation before
publishing updates.

The owner reset the displayed version from 1.6.0 to **0.9.0** on 18 October 2026,
with build 15. Game content and saves are retained. Earlier releases and tags stay
in the history; 0.9.0 becomes the latest release. Small updates continue as 0.9.1,
0.9.2 and so on. This is a numbering reset, not a checkout of older game code.

An authorised numbering reset uses:

```bash
npm run release:prepare -- --reset-version 0.9.0 --reason "Owner requested pre-1.0 numbering"
```

Stage its `tools/version-resets.json` audit record alongside the four metadata
files. CI accepts that exact old/new version and build transition
only with one newer build/cache and no bundled gameplay changes. Never reset
builds, reuse an existing version tag or edit published history.
The pre-push hook and CI reject player changes without a suitable version/build bump. After all seven
test jobs pass on main, it creates the version tag and a GitHub release with
generated notes. Repeated runs skip existing releases, older main runs skip
publication, and existing tags are never moved. Release-tool-only changes use the isolated Git fixtures and both phone
release/footer/offline checks locally; the complete browser, security, rule and
performance suites still gate publication in CI. Gameplay changes retain the
full local browser and balance checks. Only that publication job receives
a write token; tests and pull requests remain read-only. Vercel continues to deploy
main on push, independently of the later GitHub release publication.

The app must be served over HTTPS for the service worker (offline mode) and "Add to Home
Screen" to work. Vercel does this by default.

**Reports (optional)**

Players can turn on **Send reports** in the Menu: a short note when a run ends and when
something breaks. No account or hero-name field is sent, but seeds and error details can
include user-supplied text. The notes go to `api/telemetry.js`, a Vercel
function that counts them in an Upstash Redis database; `dashboard.html` shows real players'
win rates beside the bot's figures in the balance table below. To switch the store on, add the
**Upstash for Redis** integration to the project from Vercel's Marketplace (it sets
`KV_REST_API_URL` and `KV_REST_API_TOKEN`), or set `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` and
`UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN` by hand, and redeploy. Without them the function answers and keeps
nothing, and the dashboard says no store is set up.

Only aggregate counts and opaque crash signatures are public. Individual reports and error
details remain in the private Redis store. Reports are limited to 6,000 UTF-8 bytes and 60
per address per minute; short-lived rate keys use a keyed digest instead of storing the IP.
Browser requests from another origin are refused. These are abuse controls, not proof that
a reported run is genuine: this public endpoint does not authenticate players.

## Security

Save codes are untrusted data. Imports check dungeon dimensions, core field types and ids,
reject prototype keys and excessive nesting, and leave the current hero intact on refusal.
Item validation covers carried equipment, floor loot and trader stock. Belt counts are
escaped as text and attributes as an additional defence against malformed quantities.
Legacy Hall migration rejects inherited class IDs; progress counters tolerate malformed
stored values without changing valid records.
Codes/files are limited to 24 MiB before decoding and 16 MiB after decoding, including gzip
expansion. Valid game numbers are preserved; this is not an anti-cheat system. Avoid putting
personal information in seeds or shared saves. Browser storage and save codes are not encrypted.
Exported codes omit the local recording and its starting memories; recordings remain in
the browser's own save and imported recording context is discarded before persistence.

The deployment applies a restrictive Content Security Policy, blocks framing by other sites,
and disables camera, microphone and geolocation access. The service worker excludes API and
cross-origin requests. Development tests and tools are excluded from deployment.

Run `npm run test:security` for adversarial import, decompression, telemetry and local-server
checks. The browser security tests also cover markup injection and API-cache isolation.
See [security policy](SECURITY.md) and the [20 October review](docs/SECURITY-2026-10-20.md)
for scope, findings and remaining operational limits.

## Run locally

With Node 24, use the included server, which binds only to this computer and
refuses hidden files and paths outside the project:

```bash
npm start
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:4173 in a desktop browser. For phone/tablet testing, use the
deployed site or a Vercel preview. If you choose another static server, configure

directory server can expose local configuration and development files.

## Tests

```bash
npm run typecheck     # JSDoc types, via tsc; nothing is compiled
npm run check:agents  # reject missing or stale AGENTS.md guidance
npm run check:art     # reviewed art sources/exports and offline inventory
python3 art/bestiary/export.py --check  # reproduce bestiary exports without changing them
python3 art/portraits/export.py --check # reproduce current class/keeper paintings
npm test              # guidance, art, types, generator, sprites, security, APIs, narration, voices and rules
npm run test:release   # automatic versions and publication safeguards in isolated Git fixtures
npm run test:security  # adversarial save/import, telemetry and local-server checks
npm run test:browser  # Playwright suites on phone and small-phone viewports
npm run test:webkit  # focused WebKit checks; CI installs this browser
npm run test:all      # both
PUZZLES=solve PUZZLE_REWARD=potion FIT=1 DIFF=normal SEEDN=40 node test/playtest.js fighter 10  # healing choice when affordable; gold|gear also valid
npm run playtest      # 40 simulated runs per class, all six classes (240 runs)
PERF=1 npx playwright test --project=perf --workers=1  # performance budget, run alone
```

The browser suite drives the real game in headless Chromium: the core loop, dungeon features,
the trader, the story layer, the endgame, and an interface pass that asserts every
control clears the 44px touch guideline, that readable text clears 4.5:1 contrast, and that a
crowded level meets its frame-rate budget under simulated CPU slowdown. Runtime depends on
the machine. CI runs the phone suite in three shards, the small-phone suite and a separate
performance job on pushes to `main` and pull requests. A separate WebKit job runs on one worker and checks the
mobile flows tagged `@mobile`: first-tap music and mute choices, map fog, portrait
expansion, puzzle choices/retries/rewards/save transfer, quoted-name and compressed-save safety, touch controls and save/resume after navigating away. CI uses
Node 24, a read-only repository token and checkouts without persisted credentials. The default browser
script uses Chromium so it works with the locally preinstalled browser. WebKit tests
supplement the [real iPhone checklist](docs/DEVICES.md); they do not verify physical
Safari, speaker output or OS tab eviction. `npm start` serves the game locally on
port 4173 with no server dependencies.
Playwright is pinned to 1.59.1: 1.56's WebKit has a confirmed
[reload crash](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/issues/37766), fixed in 1.57.
Use Node 24 for the included server, tests and Vercel functions (`package.json` pins the runtime).

`npm run playtest` loads the real rules headlessly and plays complete runs with a bot that
fights, casts at range, retreats when hurt and rests when safe. It reports win rate and average
depth per class, which is how the balance below was tuned rather than guessed at. Runs take
each class's two paths in turn and report how each did; `HEROPATH=knight` (or any path) forces
one. The default is 20 dungeon seeds with two trials per seed for each class. For a larger,
single-class benchmark:

```bash
FIT=1 DIFF=normal SEEDN=40 npm run playtest -- fighter 10  # 400 Fighter runs
FIT=1 DIFF=hard SEEDN=40 npm run playtest -- fighter 10    # Hard difficulty
FIT=1 DIFF=normal SEEDN=40 SEEDPFX=alt npm run playtest -- fighter 10
FIT=1 DIFF=normal SEEDN=40 JSON_OUT=/tmp/balance.json npm run playtest -- fighter 10
```

`FIT=1` places the best stat in the class's primary ability, as character creation does.
Use `SEEDPFX=alt` and `SEEDPFX=third` to compare separate seed sets. `JSON_OUT`
keeps exact individual outcomes, run options, seeds and trial counts; the text
summary rounds percentages. Record the revision and any other environment flags
with the command.
`EARLY=1` counts deaths below level 5; deaths before choosing a path are recorded
as `died && !path` in the JSON. Default path rates also use different background
mixes, so use matched, forced `HEROPATH` runs before deciding a path needs tuning.
The bot measures balance; it does not change the game's rules. Published win rates
below are measurements at the recorded revision, not an automatic run of the
current checkout. Re-run the relevant classes and
difficulties before replacing them, recording the revision, settings and seed sets.

Balance testing is required before shipping changes to classes, enemies, abilities,
combat, loot, economy, progression or dungeon generation. Compare the previous and
changed revisions with matching settings on Normal and Hard, covering affected classes
and paths (all six public classes, plus the hidden Spellblade when affected, for shared systems). Tune on at least two seed sets and
validate the final change on `bench`, `alt` and `third`, normally 400 runs per class,
difficulty and set. Record commands, revisions, run counts, win rates, depth, early
deaths, death causes and resource use before updating measured results. If testing is
blocked, report it and keep the gameplay change unpushed until validated.

Review balance across all six classes before each release, including affected run
lengths and ladder rungs. Existing benchmark results can be reused when gameplay has
not changed. Art, audio, presentation-only UI and documentation changes need relevant
regression checks; changes to controls, targeting, timing or visibility also require
balance testing when they affect gameplay. The full rule lives in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

`npm test` runs the headless generator checks, plus a rule suite that loads the real game logic and
exercises the edge cases that are easy to break quietly: enchanted duplicates not merging in the
pack, two-handed weapons stowing the shield, save surviving a JSON round trip with buffs intact,
dropping from a full pack, poison and buffs expiring, the Heart resisting sale, and a trader
charging the right price. The generator checks: every level for a set of seeds and sizes must be solvable
(keys before the doors they open, stairs or artifact reachable), deterministic, and internally
consistent. The sprite sheets are validated too. The same suite runs in GitHub Actions on every
push and pull request.

points everywhere: without it the same build wins about 74% on Normal, 51% on Hard, 74% and 53%
on the Long Delve. Most of that is the early floors, where it takes blows meant for the hero; a
thinner hound that fell in most runs gave nearly as much, so it is kept worth having rather than
worn down to nothing.

Answering monster tricks decides runs: a warned blow lands whatever your armour, so a bot that
ignores every warning (`NOREACT=1`) wins roughly twenty to thirty points less on Normal.
Clearing each floor first (`EXPLORE=0.8`) is the slower, safer road: on Normal it wins about
84% as a cleric, 90% as a fighter, 86% as a thief, and 70% as a mage. About one hero in twelve
who reaches the last floor on Normal dies there, about half of them to the lich itself (`LICH=1`
breaks it down). It used to be fewer than one in thirty to the lich, the rest to the floor's
ordinary creatures on the way; now the lich hits surer and harder than its floor, and its floor
holds a quarter fewer of the rest, so the last fight is the lich and the win rates held. A fallen hero's shade
(`SHADE=4` plants one on floor 4) kills about one in a hundred of the heroes who meet it, and win
rates barely move: it is a hard fight for gear worth having, not a trap. Gold carried past the last trader, and found on the
last floor, buys a ward or a blessing at the vigil lamp beside the lich's hall. Backgrounds are
rotated across runs so the figures are not one perk repeated. `DODGE=1` has the bot step back from
every ordinary blow, as a twitchy player does, and `DODGE=2` steps aside from the monsters that lunge;
stepping back still pays, but no longer beats everything. The bot is a steady player, not a
great one: it does not step back from ordinary blows, so a careful human does better.

The bot is a mediocre player, so a human should do considerably better; the mage trails on Hard
mostly because the bot kites badly, which is exactly what a mage lives on. The
test suite guards the arithmetic that matters: the boss fight must be winnable by a level 8
fighter in chain mail with a plain long sword, with a 25% margin, and every class must have a
weapon that reaches at range.

Flat damage bonuses from strength and experience scale with how long a weapon takes to swing, so
melee arms land within a couple of points of each other on damage per second rather than the
fastest weapon always winning. Missile weapons sit deliberately below them, paying for reach.
Thieves are the exception: their bonus comes from dexterity and ignores swing weight, which is
what finesse means. A ranger's Steady Aim (+1 two squares off) is for bows and slings only, and
the pack's figure counts it: it used to come with throwing knives too, so the bot (and the pack's
comparison) chose knives, and a ranger that really held a bow on its long reach with the old +2
won about 64% on Hard against the others' 57 to 59.

## Keeping documentation current

Review this README with every change and update affected features, controls, setup,
commands, tests, layout and measured balance results in the same commit. This requirement
also lives in the [agent instructions](AGENTS.md).

Edit `CLAUDE.md` when contributor guidance changes, then run `npm run docs:agents` to
regenerate `AGENTS.md`. `npm run check:agents`, also part of `npm test`, CI and the pre-push
hook, rejects a missing or outdated generated copy.
