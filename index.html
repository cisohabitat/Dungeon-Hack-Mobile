How to Play

You are alone in a dungeon generated from a seed. Descend every level, find the Heart of the Mountain on the deepest floor, and take it from the thing that guards it.

Who you are

Pick a class and a background. The class decides what you can wield and cast. The background is the life you had before the first stair: it gives you one lasting advantage, colours the opening, and decides how your story is told at the end.

Growing

Experience raises your hit points and unlocks spells on its own. On every second level the delve also teaches you something, and you choose one of three lessons. Stat gains can be taken again and again; the lasting perks only once, so late choices matter.

Encounters

Not everything down here wants to fight. On most floors something waits to be dealt with: a survivor under fallen stone, a shrine that still takes offerings, words cut into a wall, a voice in the dark that knows your name. Walk up to it or press Examine. Each choice says what it tests and how likely it is to go well — Strength: d20+2 vs 12, 55% chance — and some cost gold or blood before you roll. There is always a way to walk away, and you have to choose one: once you have looked, the moment has to be answered. Each encounter is met at most once in a run.

Your six scores

Every score matters, fighting or not. Strength lands harder blows and forces doors. Dexterity keeps you out of reach and your fingers quick. Constitution is your hit points and your stomach. Intelligence powers a mage's spells and lets anyone work out an unknown potion or scroll — choose Study in your pack rather than finding out by drinking it. Wisdom powers a cleric's prayers, spots traps before you step on them and holds your life together against the drain of the dead. Charisma changes what traders charge you and pay you, and talks you through many encounters. Your class and background give a knack for some things: a thief picks locks well, a cleric's prayers are heard, the tombwise read old writing.

The crews who went first

Four guild crews came down before you and none came back. Each level holds one page of what they left behind, up to eight in all, so a short delve holds fewer. Pick them up and read them in the Journal. Whether you carry them out is part of how your run is remembered.

Moving

The d-pad turns you and steps you one square at a time. Walk into doors to open them, into locked doors to use a matching key, and into a staircase to take it. Stairs are set into the walls, so you have to be facing them: if you step up beside a staircase the log says which side it is on, and the Use button changes its name to say what it will do — Descend, Open, Trade and so on. Hold a button to keep walking. You can also swipe on the view: left or right to turn, up or down to step. Tap the view to act on what is in front of you.

Secrets

Some walls hide secret doors leading to treasure vaults. Walk into a suspicious wall or press Use facing it to search. Thieves often notice them just by walking past. Fountains set into the walls restore you fully, once.

Gold

A hooded trader sets up shop on some levels. Walk into them to open the shop. They sell potions, scrolls, food and the occasional weapon, and buy anything you do not want at about half its worth. Gold you never spend is only a number on your gravestone.

Fighting

Everything happens in real time. Attack strikes the square in front of you. Monsters that reach you will hit back; step away, close doors behind you, or fight in corridors where only one can reach you. A red chevron at the edge of the view marks anything close on your flanks or behind you: it pulses when the thing is right beside you. A creature that wakes up growls a moment before it attacks, so turn toward the sound. Thieves move quietly, so monsters notice them late, and they deal double damage to sleeping foes.

Every swing is a twenty-sided die plus your bonus against the target's armour class, and the log shows the working: (d20 14+5 vs AC 13). A natural one always misses and a natural twenty always tells, so those say so instead of printing a sum. Monsters roll the same way against your armour class, and their blows show it too. Watch the numbers to learn what you are up against — if your bonus is barely clearing its armour, something heavier or a different approach will serve you better, and if everything is landing on you, your armour has fallen behind the depth. Turn it off under Menu if you would rather not see the arithmetic.

A Fighter can put a second light weapon — a dagger, club or short sword — in the off hand instead of a shield. Open the pack, choose the blade and press Off hand. The second blade strikes after the first but swings wilder and carries none of your strength, and holding two slows your main hand. It clears a room of soft targets faster than anything else; against something well armoured a two-handed weapon still lands the better blow, and a shield still keeps you alive longer.

Magic

Mages and Clerics have spell points that return slowly as you walk, or fully when you Rest. Cast repeats your last spell. Casting takes time, like a swing: a mage's words are quick, a cleric's prayers slower, and you cannot swing and cast at the same moment. Anyone can read scrolls from their pack.

The Heart

The Heart of the Mountain waits on the deepest level, guarded. Taking it does not end the run. Every dead thing in the mountain wakes, the dark starts producing pursuers, and you must climb all the way back to level 1 and take the stairs out. Resting is nearly impossible while they hunt you, so plan your potions before you lift it.

Survival

Walking makes you hungry. Eat rations from your pack or you will start to starve. Resting heals you fully but costs food and is impossible with enemies close by. Watch for traps in corridors.

Keyboard

W/S or arrows to move, A/D to turn, Q/E to sidestep, Space to attack, U to use, C to cast, R to rest, M map, I pack, P spells, H hero, J journal, Esc to close.

Progress is saved automatically when you change levels. Save any time from the menu. Permadeath deletes your save when you die.
