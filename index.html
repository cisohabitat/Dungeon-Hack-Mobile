How to Play

You are alone in a dungeon generated from a seed. Descend every level, find the Heart of the Mountain on the deepest floor, and take it from the thing that guards it.

Who you are

Pick a class and a background. The class decides what you can wield and cast. The background is the life you had before the first stair: it gives you one lasting advantage, colours the opening, and decides how your story is told at the end.

Growing

Experience raises your hit points and unlocks spells on its own. Every level also teaches you something: you choose one of three small lessons, a point of strength, a few hit points, a surer eye. On every even level you choose a talent instead, one of three from your class's six. Talents change how you fight — a fighter's Cleave that carries into the next of a group, a mage's Mirror Image, a thief's Shadow Step that turns a sidestep into a blow from the shadows — and each is yours for the rest of the run. Your talents and lessons are listed under Hero.

Encounters

Not everything down here wants to fight. On most floors something waits to be dealt with: a survivor under fallen stone, a shrine that still takes offerings, words cut into a wall, a voice in the dark that knows your name. Walk up to it or press Examine. Each choice says what it tests and how likely it is to go well — Strength: d20+2 vs 12, 55% chance — and some cost gold or blood before you roll. There is always a way to walk away, and you have to choose one: once you have looked, the moment has to be answered. Each encounter is met at most once in a run.

Your six scores

Every score matters, fighting or not. Strength lands harder blows and forces doors. Dexterity keeps you out of reach and your fingers quick. Constitution is your hit points and your stomach. Intelligence powers a mage's spells and lets anyone work out an unknown potion or scroll — choose Study in your pack rather than finding out by drinking it. Wisdom powers a cleric's prayers, spots traps before you step on them and holds your life together against the drain of the dead. Charisma changes what traders charge you and pay you, and talks you through many encounters. Your class and background give a knack for some things: a thief picks locks well, a cleric's prayers are heard, the tombwise read old writing.

Relics

A few pieces of gear down here have names: Grimtooth, Rustwarden Mail, the Ogre's Toll. Each is a weapon, armour or shield you could use anyway, better made and with a power of its own — striking the undead harder, drinking back health, keeping poison out of your blood. Their names show in gold, and they hover where they lie. A handful wait on the floors of every run, always ones your class can use, usually on the pile furthest from the stairs. The traders keep the rest behind the counter for anyone with the coin. Look at one in your pack to read its powers and its story.

Every relic any of your heroes picks up or buys goes in the codex, kept from one delve to the next: open it from the Hall of Heroes or the Journal's Relics tab. The ones still missing show only whether they are a weapon, armour or a shield.

Ordinary gear can carry a power too. From the second floor down, some well-made pieces are of Keenness, of Thirst, of Flame, of Warding and the like, each with one power a relic might have. Like any found gear its quality is hidden until you wear it, study it or have a trader look, and a powered piece is worth far more to sell. Everything your gear gives is listed under Hero.

Curses

Gear you find keeps its quality to itself: the pack marks it with a ?. It might be finely made, it might be ordinary, and from the second floor down it might be cursed. Put it on and you find out at once, but a cursed piece will not come off again until the curse is broken. Safer ways to know: Study it from your pack (an Intelligence check), or pay a trader to appraise it. To break a curse, read a Scroll of Remove Curse, pray at a shrine, or pay a trader to lift it. Gear bought from a trader is always what it says.

The crews who went first

Four guild crews came down before you and none came back. Each level holds one page of what they left behind, up to eight in all, so a short delve holds fewer. Pick them up and read them in the Journal. Whether you carry them out is part of how your run is remembered.

Moving

The d-pad turns you and steps you one square at a time. Walk into doors to open them, into locked doors to use a matching key, and into a staircase to take it. Stairs are set into the walls, so you have to be facing them: if you step up beside a staircase the log says which side it is on, and the Use button changes its name to say what it will do — Descend, Open, Trade and so on. Hold a button to keep walking. You can also swipe on the view: left or right to turn, up or down to step. Tap the view to act on what is in front of you.

Secrets

Some walls hide secret doors leading to treasure vaults. Walk into a suspicious wall or press Use facing it to search. Thieves often notice them just by walking past. Fountains set into the walls restore you fully, once.

Gold

A hooded trader sets up shop on some levels. Walk into them to open the shop. They sell potions, scrolls, food and the occasional weapon, and buy anything you do not want, for about a quarter of what they would ask for it. Their forge will hone your weapon or reinforce your armour, up to +3, for more each time. You can carry five of any one potion. Gold you never spend counts for nothing, not even on your gravestone.

Fighting

Everything happens in real time. Attack strikes the square in front of you. Monsters that reach you will hit back; step away, close doors behind you, or fight in corridors where only one can reach you. A red chevron at the edge of the view marks anything close on your flanks or behind you: it pulses when the thing is right beside you. A creature that wakes up growls a moment before it attacks, so turn toward the sound. Thieves move quietly, so monsters notice them late, and they deal double damage to sleeping foes.

No blow comes without warning. A monster about to strike draws back and a warning mark appears over it, turning from yellow to red as the blow comes; you also hear it. Step back or sidestep before it lands and it hits empty air. An archer drawing its bow misses if you step out of its line. A blow coming from your side or behind makes the red chevron at that edge flash fast.

Every swing is a twenty-sided die plus your bonus against the target's armour class, and the log shows the working: (d20 14+5 vs AC 13). A natural one always misses and a natural twenty always tells, so those say so instead of printing a sum. Monsters roll the same way against your armour class, and their blows show it too. Watch the numbers to learn what you are up against — if your bonus is barely clearing its armour, something heavier or a different approach will serve you better, and if everything is landing on you, your armour has fallen behind the depth. Turn it off under Menu if you would rather not see the arithmetic.

From the second floor down, goblins, rats, skeletons and bats go about in groups of two, and deeper in threes, sharing one square. You fight the one in front; when it falls the next steps up, and the square is clear only when the last is down. They draw back together and swing in a quick volley, every one of them, so a group hits hard; step away while the warning mark is up and the whole volley misses. And a spell that fills the square hits every one of them at once: a mage's Burning Hands, Lightning Bolt and Cone of Cold, a cleric's Flame Strike, and a Scroll of Fire. Lightning and cold carry on down the corridor into any group behind.

Monster tricks

Many monsters have a trick of their own. It gets a bigger violet warning mark, a deeper sound and a line in the log, and every trick has an answer — usually a step, sometimes a weapon or a spell. A few monsters have a nature rather than a trick, like the troll that grows its wounds back. Your Bestiary, in the Journal and on the title screen, writes each trick down once you have seen it, and the answer once you have beaten it.

A Fighter can put a second light weapon — a dagger, club or short sword — in the off hand instead of a shield. Open the pack, choose the blade and press Off hand. The second blade follows every blow of the first, hit or miss, but it swings wilder (wilder still when the first has missed and left you off balance) and carries none of your strength, and holding two slows your main hand. It clears a room of soft targets faster than anything else; against something well armoured a two-handed weapon still lands the better blow, and a shield still keeps you alive longer.

Magic

Mages and Clerics have spell points that return slowly as you walk, or fully when you Rest. Cast repeats your last spell. Casting takes time, like a swing: a mage's words are quick, a cleric's prayers slower, and you cannot swing and cast at the same moment. Anyone can read scrolls from their pack.

The Heart

The Heart of the Mountain waits on the deepest level, held fast by the Dread Lich: it will not come loose while the lich stands. The lich fights in three acts. At two thirds of its life it raises guards and throws grave-cold from behind them; at one third it puts out its torches and tries to drink the Heart's light to mend itself — strike it to break the rite. Bring it down, lift the Heart, and the run is won.

Survival

Walking makes you hungry. Eat rations from your pack or you will start to starve. Your wounds close on their own only up to half your life (more for the Heartsworn); past that it takes a potion, a prayer or a Rest. Resting costs food and is impossible with enemies close by. The first rest on a floor restores everything; the second does half as much, the third a quarter, and after that the dark is too close to sleep until you find the next floor. The longer you linger, the likelier something finds you asleep. The Rest button shows how much the next rest will do; with anything close it dims and says foes near, and it never drinks for you. Drink in a fight from the Pack, or in one tap: a fighter or thief has Quaff on the Cast button, and a mage or cleric carrying a healing potion they know gets a red bottle button beside the life bar. Watch for traps in corridors.

The deep takes the measure of whoever comes down. Arrive on a floor far stronger than most who reach it and what waits there is readier for you: sturdier, surer, and more often a champion. Clearing every corner still pays, just not for free.

Keyboard

W/S or arrows to move, A/D to turn, Q/E to sidestep, Space to attack, U to use, C to cast, R to rest, X to drink a healing potion, M map, I pack, P spells, H hero, J journal, Esc to close.

One life

Permadeath is on unless you untick it when you make your hero. The run is still saved whenever you change levels or put the game away, so you can close it and pick up where you were with Continue. What you cannot do is go back: Load Game stays shut, and when you die the save is erased. Untick permadeath for a gentler run where you can save from the menu and load it again after a death.

Difficulty

Choose it when you make your hero. Easy leaves more to find, and the monsters never grow with you. Normal is the delve as it was meant. Hard has sturdier monsters, thinner rests, and a lich at full strength.

Trophies

Claim the Heart and your class earns a trophy for that difficulty: four classes, three difficulties, twelve in all, shown at the top of the Hall of Heroes. Daily runs count too. Two backgrounds are earned this way. The Returned opens after any win on Normal or Hard, and The Heartsworn after a win on Hard; until then they show on the New Game screen as locked, with how to earn them.

The Daily Delve

Everyone gets the same dungeon each day, with the same hero: class, background and scores all come from the date. Eight floors, normal difficulty, one life, and one try. When it is over the title screen shows how you did, and Share copies a one-line result to paste wherever you like. Play on consecutive days to build a streak.
